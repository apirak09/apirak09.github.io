Project 1

Triple-effect NaOH Evaporator
Steam KPI and heat recovery study

Project 1 · problem statement

Can the plant reduce fresh steam without breaking the real operating basis?

Feed32 wt% NaOH
Product50 wt% NaOH
Actual basis40 m³/h
KPIkg steam / 1000 kg NaOH(100%)

Critical check: comparisons must not mix 55.2 m³/h design-flow results with 40 m³/h actual-operation results. This deck separates those two bases.

System overview

Backward-feed triple-effect evaporator: pressure and temperature are linked

EV1highest Tfinal 50 wt%
EV2middle~41.9 wt%
EV3vacuum side~36.6 wt%
NaOH solution moves backward
EV3 → EV2 → EV1
Vapor heat cascades
EV1 → EV2 → EV3

Plant reference pressures: EV1 ≈ 0.42 barg, EV2 ≈ −0.7 barg, EV3 ≈ −0.94 barg.

Why it matters: lowering one effect is not isolated; boiling point, vapor duty, and exchanger ΔT move together.

Model method

The Aspen model was treated as a plant argument, not as a black box

Property methodELECNRTL / electrolyte model for NaOH–water behavior
Physical anchorsNaOH concentration, Cp/enthalpy trends, boiling behavior
Operating anchorsEV pressures, feed temperature, product concentration, cooler target
Equipment anchorsEconomizer network C1–C6, fixed-area logic, vapor fraction constraints

The useful model is not the one that converges once; it is the one whose assumptions can survive questions from plant engineers.

Model checks

Two checks prevented the model from giving a “nice but wrong” answer

CheckRisk if ignoredDecision in this deck
Feed basisOptimization looks better because feed is higher or design-likeMain narrative uses actual 40 m³/h basis; design-flow results are labeled separately
Pressure couplingChanging one evaporator independently violates plant physicsEV1/EV2/EV3 pressure behavior is kept as a linked system
Heat exchanger ΔTRequired area explodes if ΔTlm becomes too smallAny “saving” must be checked against practical heat-transfer area
Result meaningSteam KPI improvement is mistaken for installable solutionKPI, area, controllability, and operability are reported together
Key result logic

Steam KPI changes for two different reasons — do not collapse them

Low-flow operation474 → 494kg steam / ton NaOH(100%)

When operating below design flow, fixed heat-transfer area and ΔT distribution can make specific steam worse.

C3 heat recovery sensitivity474 → 452.7kg steam / ton NaOH(100%)

At the design-basis sensitivity, adding heat recovery at C3 reduced KPI by 21.3 kg/ton.

Final engineering ruleSame basis onlyotherwise the comparison is misleading

Final ranking should compare all cases at the same 40 m³/h actual feed basis.

Heat recovery opportunity

The attractive idea was not “add more heat exchanger”; it was “increase useful ΔT where it actually helps”

Hot NaOH / vapor-side heat
→
C3 added plate HX
→
Feed preheat before evaporation

What worked: recovering heat before the evaporator reduced fresh steam demand in the simulated sensitivity case.

What still limits it: if the hot and cold streams approach too closely, ΔTlm collapses and required heat-transfer area becomes impractical.

Therefore: the next model update should report KPI and required area on the same slide, not KPI alone.

Project 1 · conclusion

The best engineering conclusion is more cautious than the best-looking KPI

Model completedAspen model can reproduce the logic of the triple-effect evaporator and heat recovery network.
Optimization direction foundC3-side heat recovery is a credible direction for reducing steam demand.
Final proof still needs one basisAll candidate cases must be re-simulated at 40 m³/h before being treated as final plant ranking.

The value of the project is not only the C3 result; it is the discipline of preventing basis mixing.

Skeptical point: a flow interlock after the exchanger may detect low downstream flow, but it may not fully describe what is happening at the pump suction, internal circulation, or shroud heat-removal zone.

Fact vs hypothesis

Do not over-claim cavitation, material attack, or human error without evidence

CandidateWhy plausibleWhat would confirm / weaken it
Low-flow heat build-upMag-drive pumps rely on internal circulation for heat removal and bearing lubricationFlow trend, trip timing, pump temperature, bearing condition
Cavitation / vapor ingressNPSH margin was thin in available calculationSuction pressure trend, noise/vibration, vapor source, operating temperature
Chemical / adhesive compatibilityPTFE/CFK bonded layer includes adhesive interfaceSurface inspection, chemical exposure history, layer orientation confirmation
Installation / alignment issueReduced clearance can increase rubbing riskMaintenance record, runout, assembly inspection, repeat damage pattern
Hydraulic check

NPSH margin is a warning flag, not a verdict

5.91 mNPSHa
5.20 mNPSHr datasheet
0.71 mmargin

Interpretation: the margin is narrow, so cavitation/vapor risk deserves attention. But NPSH alone cannot explain shroud damage unless it is linked to actual suction pressure, temperature, gas entrainment, and vibration evidence.

Recommendations

Prevention should close the monitoring-to-action gap

1 · Measure near the causeAdd or trend suction pressure / level / pump-side indicators, not only downstream FT.
2 · Define stop timingLow-flow or abnormal condition should trigger action before heat build-up becomes damaging.
3 · Verify standby logicConfirm automatic or operator changeover pathway and failed-start response.
4 · Inspect material interfaceTreat adhesive bond as its own failure mode, not only PTFE or CFK.
5 · Preserve evidenceKeep damaged shroud, bearing, and trend history tied to the same event timeline.
6 · Avoid single-cause closureClose only hypotheses that are contradicted by evidence; do not close because they are inconvenient.
What changed in my engineering thinking

The internship changed how I treat numbers

Before

Find the equation, solve it, and present the result.

After

Define the operating basis, test the assumption, and explain what the number does and does not prove.

Aspen: a convergence result is not a plant answer.

RCA: a plausible story is not a root cause.

Engineering: the first job is to protect the basis of comparison.

Final takeaway

Two projects, one operating principle

Project 1Do not compare energy cases unless the basis is identical.
Project 2Do not close RCA unless the mechanism is testable and evidence-linked.

Plant engineering is less about producing one perfect answer and more about making decisions that survive cross-checking.
